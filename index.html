Invincible Vs. (Unreal Engine)

Fighting games are near and dear to my heart and working on Invincible Vs. was no different. Within the small and adaptable team, I was a key contributor to the game's mode and tool infrastructure focusing on elements that expedited designer iteration and player learning. I was the primary dev behind its game AI systems and internal/external practice tools. In doing so I authored UI/UX widgets, engine commands and data utilities, and a modular component that could be attached to any match for easy access to a "Training mode" tool suite.

I also authored the primary mode flow used throughout the game, allowing engineering to streamline game mode creation. This "Mode Manager" allowed relevant data and UI to persist across the lifetime of player-facing modes, even during world transitions or network travel.

In the series' Part 1 remake, I worked closely with the game and AI systems, focusing primarily on accessibility and immersion. My main focus was prototyping and exploring innovative ways to play the game. This proved to be a delicate process, requiring new methods of tracking completed checkpoints and caching/preloading assets for seamless progression and to uphold the series' cinematic quality standard.

During the pre-launch dev cycle of WWII, I worked primarily with the weapons systems, animation, and game AI specific to the multiplayer modes. Some of this included supporting systems to spawn and coordinate AI agent scorestreaks (such as paratroopers), proving a weapon inspect feature for players to flex their cosmetics, and iterating on core navigation systems that improved prone movement and corpse animation/ragdolling.

My time on Vanguard was primarily the ideation and iteration on a playable prototype that later became the Arms Race mode. With a goal to allow small-scale player level creation, I expanded the feature-set of our interactive in-game actors and allow them to spawn dynamically in-game.
